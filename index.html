How Does Smell Convey Information

Follow a simplified olfactory pathway from an odor molecule’s interaction with sensory receptors to bulb output and selected receiving brain networks.

Where smell begins

Olfactory sensory neurons are cells in the lining high inside the nose. Receptors on these cells interact with odor molecules. We’ll follow how that interaction becomes a signal traveling toward the olfactory bulb.

Zoom in: What information reaches piriform cortex?

Bulb output reaching piriform cortex is distributed across many neurons and changes over time. This teaching model compresses that activity into eight simplified output groups so we can ask what happens when part of a pattern becomes unavailable.

Each value summarizes whether one modeled bulb-output group crosses an activity threshold during an observation window. A 1 means above threshold, a 0 means below threshold, and a ? means the model cannot observe that group. These are invented patterns, not measurements of real odors or individual neurons.

Selected input: Pattern A

These are three invented patterns across eight simplified signal channels, not measurements of real odors. A 1 means activity meets our model’s threshold; a 0 means it does not. A ? means that channel’s information is unavailable—not that it is inactive.

The model compares visible values with stored patterns. It does not simulate individual action potentials, map patterns to particular brain regions, or learn like a neural network.
